# Cameroon (CEMAC region) — Food Security Outlook (Synthetic, Jun 2026–Jan 2027 style)

Region: Far North and North regions (CEMAC zone, bordering Chad and Nigeria)
IPC Phase: Crisis (Phase 3) despite ongoing harvest
Primary drivers: Persistent conflict-related insecurity, elevated staple food prices (broken rice, maize) above the five-year seasonal average, post-election-related disruption easing gradually.

Prices for broken rice and maize remained above the five-year average in June, driven by strong demand following early stock depletion, supply chain disruption, and elevated transport costs, even as weakened household purchasing power dampened overall demand. Countrywide, food prices remain elevated relative to historical norms but have eased somewhat compared to the prior year as post-election-related inflationary pressure recedes.

Outlook: Harvest-driven relief is expected to be partial; Crisis-level outcomes are likely to persist in conflict-affected zones of the Far North despite the seasonal harvest, given the combination of high prices and access constraints. This pattern is broadly representative of price and access dynamics observed elsewhere in the CEMAC zone (Chad, Central African Republic border areas).
