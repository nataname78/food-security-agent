# Niger — Food Security Outlook (Synthetic Report, Feb–Sep 2026 style)

Region: Tillabéry, Diffa, Tahoua (Sahel belt)
IPC Phase: Crisis (Phase 3) in conflict-affected departments; Stressed (Phase 2) elsewhere
Primary drivers: Recurrent conflict-related displacement, below-average 2025 cereal harvest in the northern agropastoral zone, market disruption on the Niamey–Tillabéry corridor.

Households in Tillabéry are depleting food stocks earlier than the seasonal average due to reduced access to fields near conflict lines. Millet and sorghum production in the department is estimated at roughly 70-80% of the five-year average. Cereal prices on local markets are elevated compared to the seasonal norm, driven by transport disruption rather than absolute scarcity. Humanitarian food assistance is reaching a subset of the internally displaced population, but coverage gaps remain in remote communes.

Pastoral households further north report early depletion of livestock feed reserves, with herd sizes declining slightly due to distress sales. Terms of trade between livestock and cereals have deteriorated for pastoralist households since the previous quarter.

Outlook: Absent a significant improvement in access, Crisis-level outcomes are expected to persist through the main lean season (June–August), with a risk of localized deterioration to Emergency (Phase 4) in the most conflict-affected communes if displacement accelerates.
