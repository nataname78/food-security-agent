# Methodology Note — Remote-Sensing Indicators for Crop Failure Early Warning

Early warning systems for crop failure typically combine several remote-sensing and ground-based indicators:

1. NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) anomaly — deviation of current vegetation greenness from the historical average for the same period, computed from satellite optical imagery (e.g. MODIS, Sentinel-2). Sustained negative NDVI anomalies during the growing season are a strong early signal of below-average yields.
2. Rainfall estimate (RFE) anomaly — cumulative rainfall relative to the long-term average over the growing season, often derived from satellite-blended rainfall products (e.g. CHIRPS).
3. Water Requirement Satisfaction Index (WRSI) — a crop-specific index comparing water available to a crop against its water requirement across the growing cycle; values below roughly 50 percent at the end of the season are associated with severe yield reduction.
4. Land surface temperature anomaly — elevated temperatures during flowering or grain-filling stages can compound moisture stress and accelerate yield loss.
5. Market price anomaly — deviation of staple cereal prices from seasonal norms, used as a downstream confirmation signal once production shocks translate into market effects.

Combining vegetation and rainfall-based indicators with market signals allows earlier and more robust detection of an emerging crop failure than relying on any single indicator, since satellite indicators can flag a production shock 1-3 months before it is reflected in market prices or household-level surveys.
