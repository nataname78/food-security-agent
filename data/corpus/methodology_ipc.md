# Methodology Note — IPC Acute Food Insecurity Phase Classification

The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) is a five-phase scale used to classify the severity of acute food insecurity in a given area or population group:

- Phase 1 — Minimal: households are able to meet essential food and non-food needs without atypical coping strategies.
- Phase 2 — Stressed: households have minimally adequate food consumption but are unable to afford some essential non-food expenditures without stress-coping strategies.
- Phase 3 — Crisis: households either have food consumption gaps reflected by high or above-usual acute malnutrition, or are marginally able to meet minimum food needs only by depleting essential livelihood assets.
- Phase 4 — Emergency: households have large food consumption gaps reflected in very high acute malnutrition and excess mortality, or are able to mitigate food gaps only through emergency livelihood strategies and asset liquidation.
- Phase 5 — Catastrophe/Famine: households have an extreme lack of food and other basic needs, starvation, death, and destitution are evident.

Classification draws on convergence of evidence across food consumption, livelihood change, nutritional status, and mortality indicators, combined with contextual analysis of drivers such as conflict, climate shocks, and market functioning.
